Projection Insights

  • NVIDIA generates massive positive residual income ($127-135B/yr) throughout the forecast. Even with revenue growth decelerating from 30% to 7% and operating margins compressing from 58% to 48%, the sheer scale of earnings dominates the valuation. ROE remains 48-94% vs a 13.5% cost of equity — the spread is enormous.
  • AMD starts with negative residual income (earning below cost of equity on its large post-Xilinx equity base) but crosses positive by FY2029 as margins expand to 19-22%. The model sees AMD as currently priced near fair value, with upside contingent on margin expansion execution.
  • Intel generates persistently negative residual income even under a recovery scenario. The $126B equity base requires $12.3B of annual net income just to clear the 9.7% cost of equity hurdle, which Intel likely won't achieve within 5 years. The terminal value is negative, pulling the market value well below book value.

Price Trajectory Summary

  • NVIDIA: Fair value rises from $54 to $71 over 5 years (+32%), reflecting continued but decelerating earnings growth. The relatively modest price appreciation vs massive earnings growth reflects the high cost of equity (13.5%) and terminal growth deceleration priced in.
  • AMD: Fair value rises from $44 to $76 over 5 years (+70%), the strongest appreciation trajectory. This reflects the margin expansion thesis: as AMD grows into its post-acquisition asset base, ROE climbs from 8% toward 15%, creating increasing residual income and value accretion.
  • Intel: Fair value is range-bound at $8-9 throughout. Even under a recovery scenario (operating margins returning to 12%), the enormous asset base and below-cost-of-equity returns keep the stock priced at a deep discount to book value ($28/share). Substantial upside exists only if Intel's turnaround produces margins well above the assumptions used here.
Model Limitations: (1) Revenue growth assumptions are judgment-based, not regression-derived. (2) Operating margins assume gradual reversion; disruptive events (new chip architectures, trade restrictions, macro shocks) could cause step-changes. (3) The residual income model is sensitive to terminal value assumptions — the perpetuity accounts for 53% of NVIDIA's value above book. (4) Cost of equity uses historical betas that may not reflect forward risk. (5) No adjustment for optionality, real options, or strategic value of Intel's foundry assets. (6) Capital structure held constant — actual buybacks/issuance would shift equity and per-share values.
