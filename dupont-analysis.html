1. Traditional 3-Part DuPont Decomposition

ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier = (NI / Revenue) × (Revenue / Total Assets) × (Total Assets / Equity)

Key Findings — 3-Part DuPont

  • NVIDIA's ROE dominance is driven by extraordinary net profit margins (55%+) combined with improving asset turnover. Its equity multiplier is actually the lowest of the three, meaning leverage is not the source of its returns.
  • AMD's ROE collapsed from ~42% (FY2020-21) to <3% (FY2022-23) after the Xilinx acquisition inflated total assets while margins compressed. The goodwill-heavy balance sheet depresses both asset turnover (0.33-0.45x) and profit margins.
  • Intel's structural decline is visible across all three levers: margins collapsed from 29% to negative, asset turnover fell from 0.55x to 0.25x, and even its higher leverage (1.67-1.87x EM) cannot offset the operating deterioration.

2. Extended 5-Part DuPont Decomposition

ROE = Tax Burden × Interest Burden × EBIT Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier
= (NI / EBT) × (EBT / EBIT) × (EBIT / Revenue) × (Revenue / TA) × (TA / Equity)

Key Findings — 5-Part DuPont

  • NVIDIA's tax burden has increased (from ~98% retention to ~85%) as profits grow and tax shields diminish, but this is more than offset by surging EBIT margins (60%+). Interest burden >100% reflects net interest income from its large cash/securities holdings.
  • AMD shows 100% tax burden across most years (tax expense data unavailable in parsed filings, likely due to net operating loss carryforwards and acquisition-related tax effects). The interest burden volatility (54%-213%) reflects how small non-operating items create large swings when operating income is thin.
  • Intel's interest burden became extreme in FY2022-23 (344%-1801%) as operating income approached zero but non-operating gains propped up EBT — a red flag for earnings quality.

3. Advanced DuPont Decomposition (Palepu-Healy Framework)

ROE = RNOA + (RNOA − NBC) × FLEV = RNOA + Spread × FLEV

This framework separates operating performance (RNOA) from the financial leverage effect (Spread × FLEV), using net operating assets and net financial obligations.

NOPAT = Operating Income × (1 − tax rate)   |   NFO = (LTD + STD) − (Cash + Securities)   |   NOA = Equity + NFO   |   RNOA = NOPAT / avg(NOA)   |   NBC = Net Fin Expense(1−t) / avg(NFO)   |   FLEV = avg(NFO) / avg(Equity)   |   Spread = RNOA − NBC

Key Findings — Advanced DuPont

  • NVIDIA's RNOA is extraordinary: 149.8% in FY2026 and 168.9% in FY2025, reflecting massive operating returns on a relatively asset-light model. Its net operating assets are small relative to earnings because huge financial assets (cash + securities) reduce NOA.
  • Negative FLEV for NVIDIA (-0.38 in FY2026) means it is a net creditor — financial assets exceed financial obligations. With a positive spread, negative leverage actually reduces ROE below RNOA: NVIDIA's ROE (76-102%) is actually lower than its RNOA (150-169%) because of its massive cash pile.
  • AMD also has negative FLEV (-0.04 to -0.28), consistent with a net cash position. Its RNOA peaked at 66% during the FY2021 growth surge but fell to 0.8% post-Xilinx as goodwill diluted operating asset returns.
  • Intel's RNOA collapsed from 28% (FY2019) to -9.6% (FY2024) as operating losses mounted against a large and growing asset base. Its recent FLEV turned negative (-0.22 in FY2025) as it raised cash, but negative operating returns mean leverage provides no benefit.
  • The spread is the key differentiator: NVIDIA's 146% spread means every dollar of net financial obligation (if it had any) would amplify returns massively. Intel's negative spread means its operations cannot even cover borrowing costs.

Summary — Competitive Positioning

  • NVIDIA occupies a class of its own among semiconductor peers. Its combination of 55%+ net margins, >1x asset turnover, and conservative leverage produces ROE levels (76-101%) rarely seen outside of software-only businesses. The Advanced DuPont reveals that this is pure operating excellence: RNOA >100%, negative financial leverage (net cash), and returns that are actually suppressed by the cash hoard.
  • AMD is in a transitional period. The Xilinx acquisition (closed Feb 2022) tripled total assets but operating income has only recently begun recovering. RNOA is 6.8% and improving — the thesis requires continued revenue scaling against the fixed goodwill base. AMD's negative FLEV (net cash) provides downside protection but limits return amplification.
  • Intel faces a structural profitability crisis visible across every decomposition layer. Negative operating margins, sub-0.25x asset turnover on a $211B asset base, and losses that turned ROE and RNOA negative. The 5-part decomposition exposes unsustainable interest burden ratios in FY2022-23, where non-operating items masked operating weakness. The foundry turnaround requires sustained capex without near-term return — a duration mismatch that the DuPont framework highlights clearly.
